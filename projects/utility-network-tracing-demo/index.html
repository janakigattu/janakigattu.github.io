How this maps to a real Utility Network

  • Feeders are subnetworks. Each feeder is one subnetwork fed by one source, exactly as a tier defines it.
  • Switches are traversability barriers. An open switch stops a trace the way a barrier stops it in the Utility Network. Closing one changes what is energised.
  • The gap is dirty data. On Feeder C, two features look connected but share no endpoint. The trace stops there and reports it, instead of returning a confident, wrong answer.
  • Isolation is an operation, not a drawing. The trace names the switches to open, the customers who lose power, and what the tie switch restores.

The Four-Gate Load Check

Before any load, from the article: four questions, each with a verifiable answer. If any gate has no answer, the load is not ready.

  1. Which traces will people run, and what decision will each one support?
  2. Where does the source data fail connectivity and attribute checks, and do the failures cluster?
  3. Who edits the network, under what versioning workflow, and who owns each asset type?
  4. How will you prove a trace is right, against the field, not just against the test data?

The full argument is in ArcGIS Utility Network: Modernise the Model Before You Migrate the Data, and in podcast episode 3.
