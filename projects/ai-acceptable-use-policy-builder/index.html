How a good AI policy is built

  1. Name the tools. A policy that says "AI" and nothing else cannot be followed. List the approved tools by name, and treat anything not listed as not approved.
  2. Name what never goes in. Most AI incidents are a paste, not a hack. Client data, personal data, and credentials are the three categories that cause the damage.
  3. Keep a human in charge. The person who signs the work owns the work, even when an assistant drafted it. Review before it leaves the team, and always before it reaches the public.
  4. Log what matters. When AI-assisted work reaches a client or the public, keep a record of what the assistant produced and who reviewed it. That log is what turns a complaint into an answer.
  5. Review the policy itself. Tools, models, and rules change every few months. Put a review date on the policy and keep it.
