AI Assistant Rollout Planner

The pilot worked for five people. Now fifty want the assistant, and the rollout is where pilots go to die. It is not the model that breaks at fifty users. It is the review queue, the training, and the guardrails. Plan the waves before you switch the whole team on.

How this planner works, honestly: it runs entirely in your browser. There is no server, no API key, no account, and nothing you enter leaves this page. The score is a planning rule from practice, not a vendor benchmark: pilot evidence 30, data and guardrails 25, review capacity 25, training and change 20. Change any number and the plan follows.

Review capacity

At full rollout, 100 requests a day means about 20 answers to review. Two reviewers at 20 a day cover 40, so the queue stays clear.

The gaps to close first

  • No gaps. The pilot proved the hours, the gates are closed, the reviewers can keep up, and every team has a champion.

Your phased plan

  1. Weeks 1 to 2, prep: confirm the four gates, name the champions, book the training, send the announcement, set the cost ceiling alert.
  2. Weeks 3 to 4, wave 1: champions and pilot users go live, review every flagged answer, fix what the pilot missed.
  3. Weeks 5 to 7, wave 2: eight early team members join, champions answer questions in the room, review stays inside capacity.
  4. Weeks 8 to 10, wave 3: the remaining twelve join, the support channel takes the questions, and the first month is measured against the success number.

Checklist

  • Four harness gates confirmed: logging, cost ceiling, fallback, scoped access.
  • Champions named, one per team, and briefed on the fallback.
  • Training booked, two hours per user, hands on with their own work.
  • Announcement sent, support channel named, cost ceiling alert set.
  • Review rota published, so the queue never waits on one person.
  • Success number agreed: hours saved per week, measured at week 4 and week 10.
