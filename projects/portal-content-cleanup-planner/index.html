Portal Content Cleanup Planner

Every Portal grows a junk drawer: test maps from 2019, apps nobody opens, layers owned by people who left years ago. This planner counts what is in yours, names what can go, and puts the cleanup in order.

How this planner works, honestly: it runs entirely in your browser. There is no server, no API key, no account, and nothing you enter leaves this page. The counts come from you, so pull them from your Portal first: Content, My organization, filter by item type, and sort by modified date. Storage reclaimed is an estimate from the share of items flagged, not a measured number, so confirm it against the item report before you quote it.

Platform

Deletion works differently on each. Enterprise has no recycle bin.

The delete list is an estimate. The planner cannot see which items sit in all three groups at once, so it takes the smallest of the three counts: stale, no views, and shared to nobody. Run the item report to confirm the exact list before anything is deleted.

How the planner decides

Four lists, in the order a careful admin works through them:

  1. Archive: stale items that are shared or viewed. They are not junk, they are just old. Move them to an archive folder or group first, and watch for a month. Nothing is lost if someone still needs one.
  2. Delete: stale, unviewed, and shared to nobody. That combination is the safest delete list a planner can produce without seeing the items. Even so, each one gets an owner check first.
  3. Reassign: items owned by people who left. Reassign them to a named owner or a service account before they become orphans nobody can edit or delete.
  4. Retag: items with no tags. Tagging is the cheapest fix in the whole plan: ten minutes a day for a week, and search starts working again.

What deletion means on your platform

ArcGIS Enterprise: there is no recycle bin. A deleted item is gone, and the only way back is a WebGISDR restore of the whole organisation, which also loses anything created since the last backup. That is why this plan archives first, confirms with the owner second, and deletes last. Turn on delete protection for the items that must never be deleted by accident.

ArcGIS Online: deleted items go to the recycle bin for 14 days, and the owner or an administrator can restore them in that window. Restored items come back private, with no metadata, categories, or folder, so a restore is a rescue, not an undo. Items in the recycle bin still use storage, so the space only comes back after the 14 days.

Where the numbers come from

The health score is five parts of 20: stale share, no-view share, private share, departed-owner share, and untagged share. The effort is planning rules from practice, not Esri numbers: 0.02 person-days to archive an item, 0.05 to confirm and delete one, 0.1 to reassign one, and 0.01 to tag one. The phased plan is sized from those totals.
