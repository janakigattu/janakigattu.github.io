GIS Data Quality Scorecard

Every migration, upgrade, and AI pilot your team plans is priced on the data you have. Ten questions show whether that data is worth the price.

How this assessment works, honestly: it runs entirely in your browser. There is no server, no API key, no account, and nothing you answer leaves this page. Your score is calculated here, on this device, and it is not stored or sent anywhere.

1. Completeness

Every record carries the attributes the work needs, and nothing is missing its geometry.

2. Accuracy

Positions are verified against a better source, and values are checked against the system they came from.

3. Currency

An update schedule that is met, and a last-updated date on every record, so stale data can be spotted.

4. Consistency

One standard for naming, domains, and coordinate systems, checked when data is published.

5. Lineage and documentation

Every dataset traces back to its source, and the metadata matches the data as it is today.

6. Fitness for use and QA

Automated checks run on a schedule, and the known limitations are written down where users can see them.

What you get in about five minutes

Ten questions, one at a time. At the end you get a score out of 100, a red, amber, or green status for each of the six areas, your top three gaps to close, and recommended next steps.

  • Bad data is the most expensive line item in any GIS project, and it never appears on the estimate. It shows up later, as rework, as a migration that stalls, or as a report nobody trusts.
  • The result is a short list of fixes, ranked by risk, with an owner and a date for each one. Not fifty items. Five or six, the ones that matter most.
  • If you are not sure where to start, start with completeness. Count the blank required fields and the records with no geometry. That number, on its own, tells you most of what you need to know.
