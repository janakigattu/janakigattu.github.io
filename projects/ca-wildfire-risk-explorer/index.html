What you are looking at

Before you buy land, plan a development, or write a mitigation grant, check this map first. It takes thirty seconds and it can change the decision.

The map shows two things that people constantly confuse: where fire is likely to be severe (the hazard zones, which barely change) and where fire is burning right now (live activity, which changes daily). A parcel can sit in a Very High hazard zone with no fire anywhere near it today, and a parcel outside the zones can still be at risk when a fire is running. You need both layers to judge risk honestly.

How to read the hazard zones

Moderate — fire hazard is present but lower
High — fire is likely to be severe when it occurs
Very High — the most severe hazard rating

These are the adopted CAL FIRE Fire Hazard Severity Zones, effective April 1, 2024 (State Responsibility Areas plus Local Responsibility Area recommendations). They are based on fuels, terrain, and fire history — not on whether a fire is burning today.

What the live layer shows

Current wildfire activity from ArcGIS Living Atlas: active fire perimeters and incident locations as reported. Use the layer switcher at the top right of the map to turn layers on and off, and switch between the topographic and imagery basemaps.

What this map does not do

  • It is not an evacuation or emergency map. For live evacuation orders and current incidents, use CAL FIRE and your county emergency site.
  • Hazard zones are a planning input, not a property-level verdict. Parcel-level risk needs a site assessment.
  • Live data depends on reporting. A fire that started an hour ago may not be on the map yet.

Data sources

  • CAL FIRE Fire Hazard Severity Zones, adopted April 1, 2024 (SRA + LRA), ArcGIS REST service
  • USA Wildfires, current incidents and perimeters from the past 7 days (IRWIN/NIFC), ArcGIS Living Atlas of the World
  • Basemaps: Esri World Topo and World Imagery
