WebGISDR Backup and Recovery Planner

Your last good backup is the only version of your organisation you can get back. This planner shows whether that backup actually exists, what it does not cover, and how to restore it when the day comes.

How this planner works, honestly: it runs entirely in your browser. There is no server, no API key, no account, and nothing you answer leaves this page. The schedule and score are calculated here, on this device, and they are not stored or sent anywhere. The times it gives you are planning figures from Esri’s published guidance. Your real recovery time is the one you measure in a restore test.

1. Your deployment

Pattern and version. They decide which backup modes exist and what WebGISDR covers.

2. What else lives there

Caches, referenced data, extra data stores, federated servers. WebGISDR does not back up all of it.

3. Your schedule

How fast content changes sets how often a full backup runs and how often incrementals run between them.

4. Your restore

A readiness score, the gaps to close, a properties file starter, and a checklist you can print and run.

Build your backup plan

Five short sections. About three minutes. If you do not know an answer, pick your best guess and treat the result as the list of things to confirm with your admin team.

1. Your deployment

Pattern first. A replicated standby changes the schedule, because exports on the primary have to be imported on the standby.

ArcGIS Enterprise version

2. What else lives in your deployment

Tick everything you have. WebGISDR backs up portal items and settings, GIS services and settings, service webhooks, and the relational, object, tile cache, and graph data stores. Everything below needs its own backup, or it is not backed up at all.

3. How fast does your content change?

This sets the rhythm. Full backups every night and incrementals through the day is the pattern Esri documents: a full backup at midnight, then incrementals at 10:00 a.m., 2:00 p.m., and 6:00 p.m.

4. Where do the backup files go?

Backups that sit on the same machines as the deployment do not survive the failure they are meant to cover.

5. Five readiness questions

Answer honestly. These five decide most of the score, because they are the five things that decide whether a restore works when it matters.
