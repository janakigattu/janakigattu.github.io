AI Pilot ROI Calculator

The pilot saved hours. The finance question is when it pays for the build. Put the pilot’s numbers in, get the payback in months. Enter what the pilot proved, what the rollout costs to run, and what it cost to build, and this calculator gives you the payback, the annual net value, and the two levers that move the answer fastest.

How this calculator works, honestly: it runs entirely in your browser. There is no server, no API key, no account, and nothing you enter leaves this page. The value side is the hours the pilot actually saved, priced at the loaded hourly rate of the people using it, over 48 working weeks a year. The 48 weeks, the review rate, and the verdict bands are planning rules from practice, not vendor numbers. Treat the result as a planning estimate, not a budget sign-off. If the pilot never measured hours saved, measure that first: a payback built on a guess is a guess.

Sources and method

Judge the pilot on a verifiable outcome. The Harness Readiness Check from Janaki’s article Harness Engineering: The Discipline That Separates Real AI Agents from Demos (LinkedIn, 24 Nov 2026): build the harness around one small task and judge the pilot on a number you can measure. Hours saved per user per week is that number here.

Price the running bill first. The AI Cost Estimator prices the model, the human review, and the hosting on the same usage. Use its monthly total as the model and hosting line above.

Plan the rollout in waves. The AI Assistant Rollout Planner sizes the waves and checks the review capacity the rollout needs. The one-in-five review rate and the 48 working weeks a year are the same planning rules used there.

What is a planning rule, and what is a fact. The 48 working weeks, the verdict bands (6, 12, and 24 months), and the adoption sensitivity are planning rules from practice, stated here so you can argue with them. The hours saved, the rates, and the costs are yours: change them and the answer changes.
