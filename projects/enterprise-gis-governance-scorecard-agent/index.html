Enterprise GIS Governance Scorecard

Governance is invisible when it works, and expensive when it does not. Ten questions show which one you have.

How this assessment works, honestly: it runs entirely in your browser. There is no server, no API key, no account, and nothing you answer leaves this page. Your score is calculated here, on this device, and it is not stored or sent anywhere.

1. Roles and ownership

A named owner for the platform, the data, and the content, and a deputy for each one.

2. Access and sharing

Who can see what, decided by role and group, and reviewed on a schedule.

3. Standards and naming

How items are named, what metadata they carry, and who checks new items against the standard.

4. Data lifecycle

One authoritative source per dataset, update dates, and retirement for what nobody uses.

5. Content management

Portal content reviewed every quarter: stale items, broken layers, missing owners.

6. Change control

Changes tested before production, logged, and reversible.

7. Audit and compliance

Logs that show who changed what and when, and licence use someone reviews.

What you get in about five minutes

Ten questions, one at a time. At the end you get a score out of 100, a red, amber, or green status for each of the seven areas, your top three gaps to close, and recommended next steps.

  • Governance is the part of enterprise GIS nobody sees until it is missing. Owners, access rules, standards, and a paper trail are what keep a platform dependable when the people change.
  • The result is a short list of fixes, ranked by risk, with an owner and a date for each one. Not fifty items. Five or six, the ones that matter most.
  • If you are not sure where to start, start with ownership. Name one owner for the platform and one for each core dataset, and write it down where the team can find it.
